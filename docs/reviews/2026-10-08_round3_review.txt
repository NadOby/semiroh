found five issues in the consolidation. None requires reopening the underlying design decisions.

1. README §35 overstates what a failed canary means. It says: “a failure shows what the language must add, remove or change.” That contradicts corpus.md §7, where a failure can instead mean “use an opaque/specialized representation”, “too much core responsibility”, or “not relevant yet”. A failed canary is evidence, not automatically a required language change. I would change this to something like: “a failure identifies a design pressure to investigate; it may justify a language change, a library/opaque boundary, or no action yet.”

2. corpus.md accidentally promotes canaries into requirements. Two sentences are too strong: “A canary that runs becomes a corpus example” and “One that needs a feature becomes a Wanted entry.” This conflicts with “Canaries are not acceptance tests.” A runnable canary should become a permanent corpus example only if it provides a stable semantic discriminator worth retaining. A blocked canary should become Wanted only if review concludes that its missing capability is a reusable language requirement – not merely because the canary needs it.

3. Roadmap Later gives canaries scheduling authority. “The design canaries … say when a prerequisite is due” conflicts with both “Not scheduled” and the canary section’s non-acceptance status. Canaries identify blocked prerequisites and when a question becomes relevant; the roadmap/owner decides whether and when the prerequisite is due. I would use wording like “identify the prerequisites that block each probe and when those probes become relevant.”

4. README resolves part of the still-open positioning question too strongly. “SHEAR is meant for large, long-lived systems … not for small programs” is stronger than anything established in the discussion and stronger than the immediately following statement that positioning as “glue” versus “control layer” remains open. The defensible claim is that SHEAR’s distinctive machinery is expected to pay off most in long-lived evolving systems. I would avoid declaring small programs outside its intended domain before that positioning decision is made.

5. graph_ledger.md states the control comparison as a result instead of a hypothesis. “A conventional object image wins where relationships stay independent; SHEAR wins where relationships between them are common and useful” is precisely what the proposed bounded experiment is supposed to test. The surrounding section is correctly marked Provisional, but epistemically cleaner wording would be: “The hypothesis predicts that a conventional object image is favored where relationships remain independent, while SHEAR is favored where relations themselves compose extensively.”
